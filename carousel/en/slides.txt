## Slide 1

What is one vote worth?

Spain's general election of 29 November 2026

Óscar García López

November 2026

Abstract

I simulate Spain's election 100,000 times, constituency by constituency, from the polls to 10 November 2026, with the errors that the same poll summary made in past elections. If each list votes in the investiture as its group did in 2023, the PP's candidate is invested in 93.8% of the runs, the PSOE's in 4.6% and nobody in 1.6%. For the average voter, one more vote changes a seat with probability 1 in 77,000, and who is invested with probability 1 in 11.3 million, close to the odds of picking all six numbers of 49. The numbers treat every list alike and advise no one.

## Slide 2

The question

A vote matters when it changes something: a seat, and with it who governs. Spain elects 350 deputies in 52 constituencies by D'Hondt, with a 3% threshold in each.

Congress invests the prime minister (Constitution, art. 99): the candidate the King proposes needs 176 yes votes in a first vote, or more yes than no votes in a second one 48 hours later; if nobody is invested within two months, the election is repeated. My rule: each list votes as its group did in 2023, on the PP's candidate as on Feijóo and on the PSOE's candidate as on Sánchez:

Candidate | 2023 vote | Yes votes

The PP's | Feijóo, 27 Sep: 172 | PP 137, Vox 33, UPN 1, CC 1

The PSOE's | Sánchez, 16 Nov: 179 | PSOE 121, Sumar 31, ERC 7, Junts 7, EH Bildu 6, PNV 5, BNG 1, CC 1

Frente Amplio and Podemos, in Sumar's group in 2023, vote as it did; lists with no seat in 2023 vote no (in a variant, they abstain). In October 2016 and January 2020, abstentions let a candidate through the second vote with fewer than 176 yes votes; in 2023 nobody abstained.

When voting pays (Riker and Ordeshook, 1968):

R = pB − C + D (1)

where p is the chance of being decisive, B what the outcome is worth to you, C the cost of voting and D what voting is worth in itself. This study estimates p.

## Slide 3

The model

Polls: each firm's latest poll with fieldwork ending 27 October – 10 November 2026, 16 firms; the median of each list. No firm is excluded.

National error: each share moves on the logit scale with σ = 0.22, the size of the same median's errors 19 days out in 2015–2023 (43 cases), centred on zero. As σ is itself estimated, each run draws its own (a Student-t with 43 degrees of freedom).

Constituencies: proportional swing from 2023 (the 2024 European vote for SALF and to split Sumar's vote), with regional and provincial deviations (σ = 0.11 and 0.07) estimated from past swings; turnout varies too.

Seats: D'Hondt with the 3% threshold, and for each list Δ, the extra votes it needs for one more seat.

The chance that one vote is decisive is the density of Δ at zero:

p ≈ N_h / (n h) (2)

where Nh counts the n = 100,000 runs with 0 < Δ ≤ h, and h is 2% of the constituency's votes per seat. With h from 1% to 5% the national answers move by 0.3% (seat) and 0.7% (investiture). For the investiture, only the runs where that seat changes who is invested count.

## Slide 4

Does it work? Backtests

I ran the same code seven days before the November 2019 and July 2023 elections, calibrated on earlier ones only, with a rule fixed before the first run: widen the errors if under 75% of the actual seat counts fall in the 80% intervals, or under 90% in the 95% ones. Result: 22 of 26 and 24 of 26; no widening.

## Slide 7

Who holds the key

Seats are not power. A priori power (Shapley–Shubik) is the share of the orders of forming a coalition, any coalition, in which a list's seats carry it to 176. The key is the chance that, voting as in 2023, a list's votes are necessary for the investiture that happens.

## Slide 8

Why vote at all

With three or more options, no ranking rule meets all of Arrow's (1951) conditions, and every rule without a dictator can reward voting against one's true ranking (Gibbard, 1973; Satterthwaite, 1975). Spain's rule is given: is one vote worth its cost?

At 30 minutes and 10 €/h, C = 5 €. With p = 1 in 11.3 million, a selfish voter breaks even only if who is invested is worth 56.5 million € to them (Downs, 1957). If B counts everyone (Edlin, Gelman and Kaplan, 2007):

B = N b α (3)

with N = 49.9 million residents, b the gain per person, α the weight you give a stranger. Here p N = 4.4: with b = 100 €, voting pays once α passes 1.1%, about 1 in 88.

Anything missing? What was checked

The seats per constituency come from the 2026 decree and the lists from the candidacies proclaimed in the BOE. ## Slide 10

Others' votes, limits, takeaways

Persuasion barely moves votes in US general elections (Kalla and Broockman, 2018); in France, canvassing moved vote shares, not turnout (Pons, 2018). Getting people out: in European experiments an hour of canvassing yields 0 to 0.2 extra voters, about 0.06 on average (Bhatti et al., 2019). At 0.06, an hour beats 10 €/h only if who is invested is worth 1.9 billion € to you; at 0.2, 565 million €.

Limits. Polls 19 days out. Proportional swing; SALF starts from the 2024 European vote, Adelante Andalucía from a flat share. Local lists can surprise. The investiture repeats the 2023 votes: no new pacts, no abstentions like those of 2016 and 2020. The Senate is left out.

Takeaways. A seat is a far likelier prize than deciding who governs. Between constituencies the odds of deciding the investiture differ by a factor of up to 330. Selfishly, voting rarely pays; counting others, it can.
